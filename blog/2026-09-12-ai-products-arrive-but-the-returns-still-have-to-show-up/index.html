AI Products Arrive, but the Returns Still Have to Show Up

September 12, 2026

Meta has launched a personal AI agent with paid plans and a possible source of transaction revenue.

At the same time, the value-investing speaker cited hyperscaler investment of 400 billion in 2025 and 800 billion in 2026, with perhaps trillions in 27 and 28.

The products are arriving. The returns still have to catch up.

Meta Has a Product, but the Revenue Model Is Not Settled

Meta introduced Muse with a free tier and monthly plans costing $20 or $100, depending on usage.

Muse can book appointments, complete electronic forms, monitor home security cameras, and handle coding and integration work.

It will be available through iOS, Android, a website, WhatsApp, and eventually Ray-Ban Meta glasses.

Meta is also exploring taking a cut of shopping transactions completed through agents. But it has not settled on a plan.

Privacy is part of the test.

Muse runs inside an isolated Meta environment. Meta says it never sees users’ actual passwords or payment details. The agent asks permission before sensitive actions.

Users must opt out if they do not want their interactions used for AI training. Meta is also offering bug-bounty payments for certain vulnerabilities.

The launch comes as Meta faces cybersecurity concerns, skepticism about AI data centers, and pressure to show returns from its AI investments. The company also recently agreed to pay nearly $17 billion to settle a lawsuit brought by state attorneys general.

AI Spending Is Running Ahead of Proven Revenue

The speaker said Nvidia will invest 99 billion into other businesses.

His view is that Nvidia’s backing helps those companies borrow money and buy more Nvidia chips.

Against that spending, the speaker gave annualized revenue figures of 65 billion for Anthropic and 40 billion for OpenAI. He said their funding reached 200 billion over the last year.

He also said OpenAI has compute commitments exceeding a trillion.

The figures may not line up neatly. But the direction is clear: more money is going into infrastructure while the revenue needed to support it is still being built.

The Bull Case Requires Enormous Productivity Gains

The speaker attributed the bull case to Morgan Stanley.

Productivity gains could lift global corporate profits from 5 trillion to 10 trillion. That additional 5 trillion would exceed the amount invested, leaving companies with enough financial capacity to keep spending.

The risk is that AI becomes cheaper and more competitive before revenue catches up with the investment.

Cheaper AI may help users without helping every company funding the infrastructure.

The speaker said even the heads of Anthropic and OpenAI do not know the results.

He also said China might eventually lead AI because it has five times the population, more people working to win the race, and lower prices.

Stocks Already Reflect Strong Expectations

The S&P 500 has returned 10x since 2009.

The speaker put forward valuations around 20, with the current P/E ratio at 26, compared with a historical average around 15.

He described the ratio as having fallen from “29 to 30” because Google, Microsoft, and others reported large gains on their OpenAI and Anthropic stakes. That description was unclear.

Wall Street projects 32% earnings growth in 2026.

The speaker also cited the unclear sequence “42% 15 and 16% next” and said earnings could almost double over the following three years.

His own calculation was very different. He said earnings grew 6% per year over the last five years, including AI-related gains and without a recession.

He also said market capitalization and earnings are concentrated in the top 10 companies.

Strong expectations are resting on concentrated earnings and valuations above the historical average he cited.

Margins and Depreciation Could Pressure Earnings

The speaker sees two pressures on earnings.

First, operating margins are at levels he described as historically unprecedented.

Second, AI infrastructure will create depreciation. Failed investments could also create impairments.

The US 10-year Treasury is getting close to 5%.

The speaker argued that investors are disregarding 5% bonds and 5% inflation while seeking 20% annual returns through AI.

He described two possible paths.

Continued money flows could support 15% annual stock gains.

In his downside scenario, valuations and earnings would each fall 50%, producing a 75% market decline. Debt and interest rates could take the decline to 80%.

He cited nearly one trillion in buybacks, one trillion in passive investment, one trillion in 401(k) additions, and other sources of demand.

His estimate was two trillion in net investment flowing into a US market worth $66 trillion.

His stated tradeoff is blunt: gain 15% per year until a reckoning, then risk losing 75%.

Debt Risk Leads the Speaker to Value Investing and Hedging

The speaker said the US government deficit equals 25% of revenues and that one trillion goes toward debt payments.

He said government interest payments rose from half a trillion to 1.2 trillion over six years. The projections he cited put debt at 60 trillion in 10 years.

Ray Dalio said the US faces a debt crisis in three years, give or take two years.

The speaker was less certain. He said it could happen next year, five years from now, or seven years down the road.

His response is value investing and hedging.

He cited Spitznagel’s out-of-the-money options strategy, which he said returned 4,000% during the 2020 crash.

His simpler example pays 6% for a put option. If the rest of the portfolio gains 16%, that leaves a 10% return.

His alternatives include Facebook if performance continues, Amazon, Asian stocks, HP, a UK homebuilder down 80%, Prosus, and Tencent.

Over eight years, he said both his performance and the S&P 500’s performance, including dividends, were 15.3%.

His minimum objective is to be 50% up over the next 10 years, with unlimited potential upside.

Apple Raises Prices While Tesla Stays Hidden

Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo, its first foldable iPhone, starting at $1,999.

The company also introduced the iPhone 18 Pro, AirPods 5, and Apple Watch Series 12. The iPhone 18 Pro and Max cost $100 more than last year’s models.

Apple had already raised Mac and iPad prices as memory and storage costs soared.

It also provided more information about the A20 Pro chip and introduced Apple Reference Image, an AI image-checking feature.

Apple is testing a new form factor, higher prices, and more AI features together.

Tesla is taking a different approach with Optimus V3.

Nic Cruz Patane said Elon Musk described Tesla as deliberately delaying the unveiling until closer to production. Musk’s stated reason was to give competitors less time to analyze and copy the robot.

An opposing response claimed the program was delayed and argued that Tesla could hide the robot’s joints with fabric.

The explanations remain disputed.

Trump and the Fed Point in Opposite Directions on Rates

CNBC reported that President Donald Trump threatened to stop trading with countries that have surpluses with the US unless the Federal Reserve lowers interest rates.

Trump said rates should be 1% or 0.5%, rather than 4%.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh recently said rate increases could be considered and reaffirmed the central bank’s 2% inflation target.

Trump is demanding much lower rates while Warsh is discussing possible increases.

Trump also said Bombardier must build in the US to retain access to the American market.

Bombardier responded that it employs workers in more than 20 states and operates sites in 10 states. It plans to open a facility in Fort Wayne, Indiana, later this year.

The demand came before Canada imposed retaliatory tariffs on about $20 billion of US goods, following US import taxes of 50% on selected Canadian products.

Starting Earlier Reduces the Monthly Investment Required

According to Fidelity, the average 401(k) balance rose 10.5% from March through June, the biggest quarterly increase since 2020.

Average balances reached:

  • $75,200 for participants in their 30s
  • $156,800 for participants in their 40s

Workers and employers contributed an average 14.4% of pay, just below Fidelity’s recommended 15%. More than 8 in 10 participants contributed enough to receive the full employer match.

The 2026 employee contribution limit is $24,500 for a 401(k), compared with $7,500 across traditional and Roth IRAs.

At a hypothetical 7% return, investing $250 each month from age 22 would produce about $953,680 by age 67.

Starting at 40 would produce $240,672. Reaching roughly $954,000 from that later starting point would require about $990 each month.

Starting earlier leaves compounding to do more of the work.

Crypto Produced Gains, Losses, and Disputed Responsibility

The speaker at @geopoliticaleconomyreport cited Reuters as reporting that the Trump family made 2.3 billion dollars from crypto investments between November 2024 and April 2026, while investors lost 2.25 billion dollars.

He characterized the result as a transfer of wealth from retail investors and Trump supporters to Donald Trump and his family.

The same speaker said Hunter Biden launched the LAPTOP meme coin at 8:00 a.m. on the 9th of September.

He said the token rose above 222 dollars before falling to around two dollars an hour later. It lost 99% of its value, and he said 12,000 wallets lost money.

Critics accused Hunter Biden and his team of conducting a rug pull. Hunter Biden attributed the collapse to a technical error.

Across AI and markets, the unresolved question is whether revenue catches up before cheaper competition, depreciation, and failed investments pressure the returns.
