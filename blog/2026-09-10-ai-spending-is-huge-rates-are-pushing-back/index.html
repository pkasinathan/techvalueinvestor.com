AI Spending Is Huge. Rates Are Pushing Back.

September 10, 2026

Oracle reported booking more than $30 billion in artificial intelligence cloud contracts in a single quarter, pushing its total remaining performance obligations to an astounding $664 billion.

Yet during that identical three-month period, Oracle recorded negative free cash flow of $5.4 billion on capital expenditures of $28.5 billion, while the 30-year U.S. Treasury yield touched 5.35%, reaching its highest level since 2007. The defining question for equity investors is no longer verifying whether compute demand exists, but determining whether enterprise cash generation can outrun the mounting cost of infrastructure debt.

Oracle’s Surge in Bookings Meets Massive Cash Outflows

Oracle’s fiscal first-quarter operating performance demonstrated explosive top-line demand. Total revenue climbed to $19.3 billion with non-GAAP earnings per share of $1.92, comfortably exceeding consensus estimates, while Cloud Infrastructure sales surged 121% year over year to $7.4 billion. Total backlog expanded by $209 billion year over year to reach $664 billion.

However, satisfying that backlog demands staggering capital outlays. Capital expenditures reached $28.5 billion for the quarter, generating a negative $5.4 billion free cash flow deficit. Management confirmed plans to invest $70 billion in data center construction over the coming fiscal year, funded partly through a $20 billion share sale.

Credit rating agency S&P downgraded Oracle’s credit rating to one notch above junk status in July, highlighting customer concentration and an uncertain path to profitability in AI infrastructure. While shares advanced 7% on backlog enthusiasm, the company’s financial model demonstrates that scaling computing capacity consumes immense balance sheet liquidity before generating positive cash flow.

PPI Energy Inflation Cement Expectations for a Rate Hike

The macroeconomic environment is becoming increasingly hostile to debt-financed capital expenditure. August Producer Price Index data rose 0.4% month over month and 5.4% year over year, driven by surging diesel and energy expenses. Benchmark 10-year Treasury yields advanced to 4.88% while the 30-year yield touched 5.35%.

Escalating inflation indicators have pushed interest rate futures to price a 70% to 80% probability of a 25 basis point Federal Reserve rate hike at the upcoming mid-September policy meeting.

While supply chain executives caution that borrowing costs are compressing enterprise margins, stubborn energy inflation leaves monetary authorities little room to ease. When high-yield corporate spreads widen and safe sovereign debt offers over 5%, equity multiples applied to capital-intensive growth platforms face relentless valuation compression.

Physical Power Constraints and Enterprise Software Traction

Physical power availability continues to govern the pace of real-world computing deployments. Specialized operators are expanding on-site power generation to circumvent utility queue delays. Fuel cell developer Bloom Energy advanced 11.2% following major commercial wins with AWS and CoreWeave, while IREN gained 8% as institutional investors evaluated its powered data center campuses.

In software, commercial adoption is beginning to demonstrate tangible operational efficiency. Salesforce Chief Executive Marc Benioff highlighted early enterprise successes with its Agentforce platform, citing a major technology customer that resolved over 60% of technical support inquiries autonomously using AI agents without human intervention.

Demand for accelerated computing and intelligent software is indisputable. However, as long-term borrowing costs hold near multi-decade highs, technology operators cannot rely on cheap capital to finance execution mistakes. Every data center campus must generate attractive cash returns on invested capital.
