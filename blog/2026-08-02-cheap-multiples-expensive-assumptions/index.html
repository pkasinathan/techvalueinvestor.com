Cheap Multiples, Expensive Assumptions

August 2, 2026

What does Samsung’s 3.2x forward PE 2027 actually mean if the forecast behind it requires roughly $1.25 trillion in operating profit over three years?

Marcos Milla ranks Samsung at 3.2x forward PE 2027, Micron at 5.2x, and NVIDIA at 16.2x. Samsung and Micron appear remarkably cheap at first glance, but a valuation multiple is only as dependable as the earnings denominator sitting beneath it. When projected earnings incorporate unprecedented assumptions about multi-year global spending, low multiples often signal peak cyclical expectations rather than mispriced bargains.

Samsung’s Multiple Embeds an Extraordinary Spending Forecast

Samsung is currently expected to generate roughly $1.25 trillion in operating profit over the next three years. According to industry analysis, reaching that earnings threshold would require at least $40 trillion in cumulative global AI spending, or approximately $13 trillion annually. By comparison, total worldwide information technology spending currently stands at approximately $7 trillion.

That discrepancy does not automatically prove the forecast will fail. It does demonstrate that Samsung’s 3.2x multiple reflects an extraordinary macro assumption rather than an ordinarily cheap business. For operating profits to expand to that magnitude, enterprise AI adoption would have to trigger an unprecedented expansion in global hardware budgets, permanently diverting capital away from other corporate priorities.

Wall Street consensus models now underwrite aggressive five-year annualized revenue growth rates across the supply chain, including 54% at Micron, 48% at SK Hynix, 44% at AMD, and 31% at Nvidia. TSMC is expected to grow 24%, while equipment makers Applied Materials, Lam Research, ASML, and KLA range from 18% to 20%. The market is already underwriting a broad, uninterrupted, and historically unprecedented hardware buildout.

Physical Scarcity Cannot Guarantee Multi-Year Peak Margins

The counterargument is visible in the physical semiconductor market today. Memory demand heavily outstrips current fabrication capacity. Producers are actively allocating supply, demanding prepayments, and raising contract prices. DigiTimes reported that the three major memory suppliers have pre-sold their available DRAM and high-bandwidth memory capacity for all of 2027, while suppliers can satisfy only 60% to 70% of requested customer allocations.

Hardware constraints extend across consumer devices as well. Apple is facing major MacBook Air delivery shortages amid memory and advanced packaging bottlenecks, with many new customer orders delayed into September. Yet while near-term physical scarcity is indisputable, its duration and financial impact remain deeply uncertain.

Cyclical businesses routinely appear cheapest on forward multiples precisely when expected earnings are reaching their cyclical peak. If the cycle cools before those projected profits materialize, the denominator collapses and the apparent valuation bargain vanishes. A low forward multiple does not eliminate uncertainty; it simply buries that uncertainty inside the forecast.

Pabrai’s Rule: Protect Cash Flow Before Buying Optionality

Mohnish Pabrai places memory and artificial intelligence directly into the too-hard pile. While he acknowledges that advanced memory fabrication is protected by massive scale, thousands of patents, elite engineering talent, and proprietary manufacturing expertise, he stresses that predicting multi-year competitive leadership remains impossible.

His preferred alternative is Kaspi. The business produces $2 billion in annual cash flow, trades at roughly five to seven times cash flow, and offers a dividend yield approaching 10%. The Kazakhstan core operation continues to grow stably, while its planned Turkish expansion represents an unpriced moonshot. In Pabrai’s framework, durable core cash flows provide complete downside protection even if the speculative expansion fails entirely. The uncertain venture is optionality, not a required condition to justify the purchase price.

Samsung at 3.2x forward earnings may promise greater upside, but that multiple depends on a memory cycle whose scale and longevity are heavily contested. Kaspi’s valuation is tied to cash flows that already exist in cash.

Sovereign Yields Raise the Hurdle for Cyclical Forecasts

The macro environment adds another layer of discipline to equity valuations. The 10-year Treasury yield sits near 4.6%, while the 1-year yield offers 4% and the 30-year bond trades above 5.20%. When government bonds offer material, risk-free yields, equities dependent on distant or highly cyclical forecasts must clear a substantially higher hurdle rate.

Investors who rely on peak cyclical earnings assumptions risk painful multiple compression if capital expenditure decelerates. True value investing demands cash flows that survive scrutiny today, leaving speculative growth as optionality rather than a necessary condition for investment survival.
