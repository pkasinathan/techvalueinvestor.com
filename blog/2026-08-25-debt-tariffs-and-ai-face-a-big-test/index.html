Debt, Tariffs and AI Face a Big Test

August 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Gold has climbed for five consecutive weeks, Bitcoin touched $80,000, and total U.S. federal debt surpassed $40 trillion as investors seek protection against fiscal deficits, currency depreciation, and interest rate volatility.

The take

  • Trade friction is intensifying across North America.
  • Financial markets remain heavily concentrated around technology earnings.

Gold has climbed for five consecutive weeks, Bitcoin touched $80,000, and total U.S. federal debt surpassed $40 trillion as investors seek protection against fiscal deficits, currency depreciation, and interest rate volatility.

At the same time, international trade tensions are escalating rapidly, and technology markets face a pivotal test as Nvidia enters earnings season carrying historically elevated expectations.

Fiscal Deficits and Currency Pressures Lift Safe Havens

Gold touched a three-month high following weekly inflows of $6.4 billion into physical bullion ETFs, representing the third-largest weekly allocation on record. Bitcoin advanced 2% on Monday following a 22% weekly surge, while the U.S. dollar index dropped to a three-month low after recording its third weekly decline in a month.

The primary catalyst is expanding sovereign debt issuance. July’s federal budget deficit reached a five-year peak, pushing total national debt beyond $40 trillion. In response, long-term borrowing costs surged, with the 30-year Treasury yield climbing to 5.34% from 4.82% in late June.

The Treasury Department announced plans to expand bond buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion, potentially utilizing its $950 billion to $1 trillion Treasury General Account to absorb long-dated issuance. Yet yields quickly rebounded following an initial decline, indicating that market participants view debt repurchases as insufficient to counter structural supply. Citadel strategist Nohshad Shah cautioned that while bond purchases might temporarily stabilize yields, they risk exacerbating dollar weakness, creating renewed inflationary pressures that could force the Federal Reserve to raise rates. Interest rate futures now indicate a 56% probability of an October rate increase.

Trade Retaliation and Geopolitical Realignments

Trade friction is intensifying across North America. Canada announced retaliatory tariffs targeting $19.94 billion in U.S. goods, directly matching Washington duties dollar for dollar. The tariffs span more than 700 consumer and industrial goods, raising steel levies from 25% to 50% while imposing duties between 15% and 50% on dairy, seafood, appliances, paper products, and clothing starting September 8.

President Donald Trump threatened additional counter-tariffs on Canadian automobiles, parts, and industrial steel, raising concerns that cross-border supply chains could face severe disruption.

In energy markets, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent introduced an expanded sanctions framework against Iran targeting digital assets, aviation, and shipping networks across roughly 60 entities and vessels. However, crude oil prices fell roughly 4% after reports surfaced that diplomatic backchannels had proposed an agreement regarding navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, with U.S. naval forces clearing maritime mines. Lower oil prices temporarily relieved headline inflation fears, pulling the 10-year Treasury yield down from 4.72% to 4.62%.

High Expectations Dominate AI Technology Leaders

Financial markets remain heavily concentrated around technology earnings. Nvidia and Tesla each recorded over 1.8 million options contracts in a single trading session ahead of Nvidia’s earnings and PCE inflation data, while markets awaited Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh’s policy address at Jackson Hole.

Raymond James raised its Nvidia target to $352 from $330, maintaining a Strong Buy rating. Ancillary infrastructure holdings attributed to investor Leopold Aschenbrenner reflected strong pre-earnings momentum: Bloom Energy advanced 5.1%, while nuclear and utility suppliers Oklo, BWX Technologies, Cameco, Vistra, and Constellation Energy recorded steady gains.

However, elevated valuations leave little room for operational delays. Technology stocks must continue delivering extraordinary revenue growth to justify multiples in an environment where benchmark borrowing costs remain stubbornly elevated. Investors are navigating a complex landscape where record fiscal debt and trade barriers compete directly against corporate technological optimism.
